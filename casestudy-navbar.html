Background

Global navigation bar failure.

With rapid acquisition-driven growth, 5paisa emerged as having multiple overlapping products and services. Their website (5paisa.com) reflected that complexity. It suffered from complex navigation that forced users into multiple disparate flows. It consisted of a wall of links having 119+ entries and poor categorisation, 3–4 steps to reach core product, vague labels, segmented user journeys, outdated interaction and visual design.

During discovery, this global navigation turned out to be a major pain point for users. Users couldn't efficiently access intended content and ended up going through unnecessary flows. Confusing IA was the main cause of decision paralysis.

Product Audit

Deep-dived into analytics to identify metric crisis areas. Mapped 119+ legacy links to quantify the full scope of the architecture problem.

Taxonomy misalignment

Users consistently skipped "Technology" when looking for trading tools — the label didn't match their mental model of where a trading platform should live.

Category confusion

Clicking "Stocks" or "Mutual Funds" showed static market data, not a trading portal. The mismatch caused repeated pogo-sticking behaviour and eroded trust.

Labelling friction

"Resources" was non-descriptive and actively avoided. High-value tools were hiding behind the worst possible label — invisible in plain sight.

Power user friction

BTST, Penny Stocks, and live analytics were buried in static product folders — significant friction for traders who need time-sensitive data without transactional clutter.

Intent collision

Long-term investors and active traders shared the same navigation path. No separation between "I want to invest" and "I want to trade" created constant intent collision.

Depth overload

Reaching any core product required 3–4 clicks. Each nesting level added cognitive cost — users gave up or used search as a workaround rather than navigating.

Tab 01 · Long-Term Wealth Creation
The "Invest" Tab
Dedicated to strategic, long-term investment products — a clean entry point to wealth-building instruments, completely free from active trading noise.
  • Houses Stocks, Mutual Funds, IPOs, ETFs, and US Stocks
  • Contextual right column with high-intent calculators (SIP, ELSS)
  • Completely separated from active trading to prevent cognitive collision
  • Brokerage and Stock Screener tools surfaced prominently
Tab 02 · Active Execution & Speculation
The "Trade" Tab
Built for high-frequency, active instruments — cleanly separated from investment avenues so experienced traders execute without drowning in informational noise.
  • Houses Futures & Options and Commodity instruments
  • Tools column shifts to surface Margin and MTF Calculators
  • Active execution pathway distinct from long-term Invest tab
  • Reduces cognitive collision between novice and expert flows
Tab 03 · The Taxonomy Correction
The "Products" Tab
Completely retired the ignored "Technology" label. Proprietary platforms are now organized under a word users actually understand — driving immediate recognition.
  • Retired the underperforming "Technology" category entirely
  • Trading App, Trader's Terminal, FnO360, Scalper Terminal front and center
  • Colour-coded iconography with micro-copy badges for instant recognition
  • Taxonomy now aligned with real user language and mental models
Tab 04 · The Real-Time Utility Hub
The "Markets" Tab
Pulled 25+ live analytics out of static transactional folders into a dedicated real-time hub — serving power users without cluttering casual investors.
  • 25+ live analytics and directory links centralized in one hub
  • Two-tier layout: left submenu populates data grids on the right
  • Market Today, Top Gainers/Losers instantly accessible
  • Time-sensitive data separated from static product folders

Reflection

What this project taught me.

What worked

Anchoring every decision in data first — the analytics audit gave the project authority that opinion-based feedback couldn't challenge. When a stakeholder pushed back on removing "Technology," the click data ended the conversation.

What I'd do differently

I'd run tree testing earlier. Validating the new taxonomy with users before high-fidelity mockups would have surfaced edge cases sooner — particularly around the Invest vs. Trade split for users who do both.

What I learned

In a complex, high-stakes financial product, minimising choices is the single most powerful way to build user trust and accelerate conversion. Design's leverage is highest at the IA layer, not the visual layer.

What surprised me

How much of the problem was labelling, not structure. Renaming one tab from "Technology" to "Products" was the single highest-impact change in the entire redesign — and it took 30 seconds to implement.
